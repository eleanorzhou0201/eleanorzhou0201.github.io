About

Hello! This is Lizhuo Zhou, and I also go by Eleanor.

I did my M.S. at Department of Statistics and Data Science, Northwestern University, where I worked with Prof. Kota Saito at California Institute of Technology.
Previously, I obtained my B.S. in Statistics from Fudan University in 2024.

My research interests lie at the intersection of statistical inference, artificial intelligence, and machine learning. I am particularly passionate about the structures behind learning and uncertainty, and developing mathematically-grounded methods for complex, high-dimensional, and dynamically evolving systems.

I am drawn to research that bridges rigorous theory with meaningful applications. Specifically, I appreciate the elegance of theory and the insights it offers into the underlying mechanisms of real-world phenomena.

Notes & Expository

Informal notes and writeups on various topics (mostly probability/stats/generative models-related stuff). Check out below for further details.

Optimal Transport and Statistical Learning

Notes on Wasserstein geometry, gradient flows, and their connections to modern generative modeling.

Decomposing VAEs and Diffusion Models: The Underlying Logic

An expository on VAEs and diffusion models, highlighting their shared variational foundations and hierarchical structures.

Classical Probability Models and Mathematical Statistics

Undergrad notes on classical probability models and some problem-solving tricks.

Finding UMVUE

A brief overview of standard 4.5 ways for finding UMVUEs.

Connections of Common Distributions

Regression Analysis: From a Geometric Perspective

A comprehensive review of regression analysis.

Statistical Decision Theory and Inference

Undergrad notes on statistical inference, spanning over point estimation, hypothesis testing, optimality criteria, and their decision-theoretic interpretations.
